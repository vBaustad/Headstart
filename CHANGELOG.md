# Changelog

## 0.10.0

- **Skip quest (new; Routes, Settings; off until you turn it on).** Right-click a step in RestedXP's guide and pick **Skip quest**: every step of that quest is skipped at once - the pick-up, the kill or loot step, the hand-in, and the "Go to" steps that lead to them - for the quest a launch crowd makes hopeless (one spawn with a queue, its items looted bare). A step that also serves another quest keeps going; only the skipped quest's lines in it count as done. The quest is not abandoned, and RestedXP won't take or hand it in for you meanwhile. One line in chat says what went ("Skipped Darkness in the Glade: 4 steps."). Right-click a skipped step for **Undo skip**, or `/headstart unskip` for the last one; `/headstart skip` skips the quest of the step you're on. Skips are kept per character and guide, through a /reload.
- **Talent builds (new; Bars & windows page).** A levelling build for each of the nine classes, made on Forever's own talent trees (read from the game's data - Forever changed many talents): its three trees with every talent's points, and what to take at each level under them. Pick any class to look at. **Follow on this character**, and the talent points notice says which talent the build takes next - "2 talent points to spend - next: Deflection 2/5" - and its tooltip the next ones; it reads your own talents from the game, so a point taken out of order still counts. The builds are Headstart's own suggestions, not tested to 60 on every class.
- **Level pace (new; Routes, Settings; off until you turn it on).** A small box under the RestedXP guide: your level, the XP still missing and the time to level at your pace (XP over your last 10 minutes of play, time AFK or dead left out), and what grinding here pays: XP a minute from your own last kills within 200 yards or in this subzone. While a RestedXP step works on quest objectives, it watches what the step pays - the quests' reward XP plus the kill XP you got meanwhile - and once it has run five minutes and pays under half of what grinding here would have, or has cost you two deaths, it says so in one line, once per step ("Harpies: 14 min for 440 XP so far. Grinding here pays more: skip it?"). It never skips anything for you. When a coming step needs a level (a `.xp` line), it says how far short you are and about how many kills here that is. With the guide window hidden, the step line goes to chat instead.
- **Quest mob markers (new; Journey page, a card on All features; off until you turn it on).** A sword on the nameplate of an enemy you still need for a quest - one to kill, or one that drops what a quest wants - from the game's own quest lines on the unit, and on the mobs your RestedXP step names (its `.mob` lines). Only living enemies: quest givers show the game's own ! and ?. The icon stands beside the plate's health bar, or over the name where none shows. With Questie's nameplate icons on, Questie's are shown and these are left out. (Through the betas it marked the NPC a step says to talk to as well, and could turn the game's friendly NPC plates on for it; plates it turned on are put back as they were.)
- **Quests on NPC tooltips (new; Journey page, a card on All features; off until you turn it on).** Hover an NPC: its tooltip lists the quests it has for you - ! to take, ? to hand in. The game says what an NPC offers only once you talk to it, so two things are told: your RestedXP route (the steps ahead that take or hand in a quest at that NPC, marked *route*), and what each NPC offered when you talked to it, remembered for all your characters and listed until you have done the quest or taken it. A quest to take is coloured by its level where the NPC said it. Not in combat. With **QuestieDB** installed - the Questie team's open quest database, an addon that works without Questie - every quest an NPC has for you is listed, talked to or not: the ones your level, race and class can take now, with the quests before them done. With Questie itself, which lists an NPC's quests on its tooltip, these are left out while Questie's are on, so nothing shows twice.
- **Quest item buttons (new; Journey page, a card on All features; off until you turn it on).** The items in your bags that a quest gave you as its reward and that you use - Really Sticky Glue, a Sprouted Frond; not food and drink, potions, bandages, recipes or gear - as buttons on screen, so they needn't be dragged onto your bars. A click uses one, as from your bags; its cooldown and how many you have show on it. Rewards are known from when this is on; **Quest items too** (off until you turn it on) adds the items the game counts as quest items, a vial to fill, a sapling to plant. An item already on your action bars gets no button, and Shift+right-click takes one off ("Show them again" brings them back). Size, a row or a column, and Arrange (`/hs move`) to place them. The buttons change out of combat.
- **World marker (new; Journey page, a card on All features; off until you turn it on).** Your map pin, shown in the world: a mark and how far it is in small letters, with an arrow at the screen's edge while it is behind you - and only within the distance you set (400 yards to begin with). The game works out where your pin is on the screen but Forever doesn't draw its own marker there; Headstart draws one. Put a pin with Ctrl+click on the world map (tracked for you: the map puts it untracked, and only a tracked one has a spot on the screen), or `/headstart way 45 62` in the zone you're in (`/headstart way` alone takes it away). A quest you set the game to track is shown the same way. **Follow RestedXP's arrow** puts the pin where RestedXP's arrow points, the step you're on, every time - as Naowh Forever does; nothing on a hearth step or where RestedXP has no place to go, and a pin the game takes away before you're there is put back. Where the game has the step's NPC within 60 yards of RestedXP's spot - the giver of a quest to take, the NPC a done quest is handed in to - the pin goes on the NPC instead (RestedXP's spot is the guide's, and on Forever an NPC may stand some yards beside it). Where the NPC the step is at has a nameplate on the screen (friendly NPC plates on, in the game's settings), the marker stands right over the plate - over the NPC's "?" or "!" - and stays while you stand there, where the game takes the pin away; elsewhere it is on the pin's spot, which has no height. Hover the mark: its tooltip names the NPC of the RestedXP step and the step's quests - to take, to hand in, to do - (or the quest you track, or your own pin) and how far; a click still goes through to the world behind it. `/headstart way log` says what it chose and why, the last fifteen times. A map pin has no height of its own - the game keeps its spot at yours, and Forever tells an addon no height that could fix it - so its mark moves with you as you jump. While Glimmer lights the step's NPC it steps aside - the sparkles and the quest over the NPC say the same. It fades in and out, never snapping, and so does Glimmer's light.
- **Arrange: snapping you can see, and anchoring where you choose.** While you drag, the element stands where it will be let go, a line runs along the edges it lines up with, and the element it lines up with is outlined in blue. An edge or the middle lines up with the same of another element or of the screen, or goes beside another with a gap between (Gap: 0, 2, 4 or 8 on the top bar); a snap that would lay it partly over another isn't taken (inside a larger one, a text on a bar, is). Hold Alt for no snapping. Hold it over another to anchor it: let go, and a small window asks where it goes - above, below, left or right of that one, lined up at either edge or the middle, on it, or where it was - each shown as you point at it; Esc or Cancel leaves it as it was. Before, it snapped only as you let go, any edge to any other, and anchoring left it lying over the other element. Arrange's own bar (Grid, Snap, Done) can be dragged out of the way, and opens at the bottom of the screen where at the top it would lie over an element, which then couldn't be taken hold of.
- **Swing timer: the ranged bar in the main hand's place, bars that fade between swings, and far fewer settings.** The ranged bar now stands where the main hand's does - you don't swing and shoot at once: the one swinging shows, else the one you used last. Visible has a fourth choice, *Faded between swings*: the bars stay in their place, see-through until they swing. The Combat bars page lost what needn't be a choice: the preview runs by itself while the page is open; Frame, Texture, Text and Font come with the preset; out of range is always shown; the range text is sized with the bars' text; and Move bars, Width, Height and each bar's own switch gave way to one **Arrange** button on each card, where every bar is placed and sized. What you had set stays as it was.
- **Flight timer: a card of its own, with Land in it.** Where you fly to in large letters and where from under it (by way of the first stop, where there are stops); at the left a ring round your portrait - or your side's flight mount, with the portrait off - that fills as you fly; at the right the time left and the clock time you land; and at the right end, after a dotted line like a ticket's stub, **Land**, which lands you at the next flight point on the way (its tooltip names it) and lights under the mouse. What's going on - timing a first flight, landing, landing at the next stop - is said in short where the landing time stands, and at length in the card's tooltip. On a direct flight Land is there but quiet: there is nothing to land at before the end. Blizzard's own Land button is see-through while the card shows, and back as you land. The ring is the game's own cooldown sweep: it draws itself, where the old bar was moved by Headstart as you flew.
- **Arrange shows every combat bar as it is.** The cast bar, the ranged swing bar and the rest each show a sample while Arrange is open, without the unlocked glow - before, a bar with nothing to show was an empty outline beside the others.
- **Character setup's menu holds only its pages.** "Whose is what" stood under it as three entries with icons and read as more settings; it is said in the page's corner now: bars every class's own, the interface every character's.
- **Lighter while it runs (a performance pass before release).** Each feature still does the same, with less work behind it:
  - **Quest mob markers** read a mob's tooltip once per kind of mob until your quest log changes, instead of for every nameplate at every update.
  - **The range bar and Range text** share one reading five times a second (each used to ask every item and spell for itself). How far your spells reach a target is asked once per target.
  - **Boats on the world map** are placed ten times a second, not every frame.
  - **The world marker** sets its text and position only when they change.
  - **Quests on NPC tooltips** skips hostile mobs and finds hand-ins through an index. (A neutral quest giver, as in Booty Bay or Gadgetzan, can be attacked but is not hostile: its quests still show.)
  - **Quest item buttons** read only the action bar slot that changed and don't lay out unchanged buttons again.
  - **The cast bar** no longer re-applies its whole look at the end of every cast.
  - **Glimmer** asks for its cursor file IDs once.
  - **Code nothing used** is removed: 15 unused functions and test hooks, two Character setup options nobody read (skipIntro, rankUp), and a value saved and never read.
- **Talent builds for tanking, healing and damage.** Next to each class's levelling build, the Talents page now has builds for a role at 60: a tank build for Warrior, Paladin and Druid (bear), a healer build for Paladin, Priest, Shaman and Druid, and a damage build for every class whose levelling build isn't already one (Fury, Marksmanship, Assassination with Mutilate, Shadow, Elemental, Fire, Destruction, Balance). All 51 points of each are in the order to take them, and each is checked against Forever's own trees. Follow one and the talent points notice says what's next.
- **The world marker no longer gets in the way of clicking the NPC.** No part of the marker takes the mouse. What it stands for (the quest to take or hand in) is now on the NPC's own tooltip when you hover the NPC the step is at. With Quests on NPC tooltips on, that tooltip already shows it, marked route.
- **The range bar in more detail.** It now says **Melee range**, **Charge range** (or the band of whichever spell of yours has a minimum reach), **Dead zone** (too close for that spell, too far for melee: a hunter's 5 to 8 yards, a warrior's before Charge), **In range** within the reach of your spells that can hit an enemy (this was "Cast range"; a 40-yard heal no longer counts), or **Out of range**, now in a clear red. Each has its own colour: Melee range green, In range blue, Charge range purple, Dead zone yellow, Out of range red (a hunter's Melee range is orange and In range green). Your melee abilities (Heroic Strike, Sinister Strike and so on) are now asked too, so standing at a mob out of a fight says Melee range, not In range. Spells only for friends (blessings, heals, seals) are not asked, so they can't hide an attack of the same reach (a paladin's Exorcism), and a long reach that isn't an attack (Mind Vision's 100 yards) doesn't make a far mob In range. The bar goes away when your target dies.
- **Simpler settings, in a quieter look.** Each settings page is one list in a rounded panel: every feature a row with its icon, its name, a line saying what it does and its switch. A feature with settings says how many ("3 settings") with an arrow: a click on its row opens them in a band under it, one to a line with a line between, and shuts them again; it is kept as you leave it, and switching a feature on opens its settings. The switch is new - a pill with a round knob that slides across, drawn pixel for pixel at the size it is on your screen, so it is as sharp as the text at any resolution and UI scale - and is the only switch: a feature's settings are checkboxes, quiet light ones (pixel for pixel too), and their whole row ticks them. The whole row lights under the mouse, edge to edge. Orange now only marks a switch that is on and the tab you are on (a line under it); the window, its menus and the list have soft shadows. The row of section links under each page's title is gone, and the foot of the window has one button, **Arrange on screen**; Tour, Move settings and Reload UI are quiet words beside it (the window's own X closes it).
- **The Levelling tab is now Routes, and simpler.** Its menu is **Next route**, **All routes** and **This run**, with **Settings** at the foot. It opens on **Next route**: the route to take now, how many of its quests are left and where to start, one button that loads it in RestedXP, your talent build, what your trainer has for you and this run beside it, and the next three routes under it (the list of every other route ran out of the window). **All routes** lists your race and class's routes by level - or every race's - with how far you are in each and which one to take; Load puts one in RestedXP, Edit opens it in the route editor, and Import a route and Share are at its foot (Share was a page of its own). The routes switch is the first row of Settings, which is now one list like the QoL pages. With the routes off, their pages say so and switch them on.
- **Talent builds moved to QoL** (Levelling, **Talents**), the trees and the order by level side by side on one screen, with "Follow it on this character" at the top and a way to the talent points notice's settings. Interface keeps the talent window's own settings, under **Talent window**.
- **New at your trainer: a small window, not a chat line.** When you level and your class trainer has something new for you at that level, a window shows each spell's icon and rank and about what they cost; point at one for the spell. Only what came at that level and that you haven't trained - a spell you skip no longer comes back at every ding - and a spell goes from it as you train it. After a fight, never in one. Its x puts it away; it moves in Arrange on screen. (Reminders, **New at your trainer**.)
- **Glimmer lights quest givers, and makes quest objects plainer.** An NPC with a quest for you - to take or to hand in, as Quests on NPC tooltips knows it - lights up as you face it - with a RestedXP guide leading, only the NPC its step is at: orange sparkles all over it, the game's own quest glimmer made large and many, with the quest's name in yellow over its own (and "+2" for more). The NPC looks as it does without Glimmer - its name small and green, no plate, health bar or level. Quest objects get the same sparkles, falling over them, with the quest's own line and its count ("Gnoll Paw: 3/8"), counting up as you loot. On the Glimmer card, under What lights up: quest givers, and each kind's text, can be switched off on their own.
- **Restock buys nothing until you set a number.** Every reagent and the ammo start at 0 (it bought the data's number - 40 Symbols of Kings, a hunter's 1000 arrows - unless you changed it). The number is how many to have: with 10 in your bags and 40 set, 30 are bought. Each reagent's row says the usual number for it.
- **Glimmer leaves your interact key alone.** Turned off in the game's settings, Enable Interact Key stays off (it was turned on again at every login); the card says when it is off, with a button to turn it on. And it sees Naowh Forever's Glimmer, a part of Naowh Forever: QoL now, and says so on its card.
- **Fewer lines in chat.** Gone: the skipped intro cinematic, each instance entered under the limits and its copy notes, the time at each level (the splits show it), the quest reward picked and "pick the one you want" (the game shows both), "joined X's group", a party member's newer Headstart, answers to your quest group request (the eye counts them), "nothing here is one of your class's spells" at a weapon master, an upgrade's line beside its window, logs packed and samples dropped, "mail goes to X". The durability warnings and the Camping 101 reminder are mid-screen only. Errors, answers to what you typed or clicked, and what was bought, sold, mailed or deleted for you still say so.
- **Fixed: the talent key in a fight below level 10.** Pressing it could be blocked ("Headstart tried to call the protected function PlayerSpellsFrame:SetAttributeNoHandler()"): Talents before level 10 changed how Blizzard's window decides its tabs. It changes nothing of Blizzard's now: below level 10 the talent key opens Headstart's Talents page (QoL).
- **Dual Wield, Mail and Plate Mail are on the Class trainer's list.** A rogue's Dual Wield at 10 (a warrior's and a hunter's at 20), Plate Mail and Mail at 40: auto-train buys them like your other spells, and the trainer reminder names them. They were left to you as "not a class spell". The list is also brought up to the game's newest build (Salvation, Revoke, Frost Blast, Arcane Wrath and others).
- **Level splits: fewer settings.** Where the splits stand is set with Arrange on screen (they no longer take clicks or drag on their own), and their background and clock are fixed; the X and Y sliders, Lock position, Background and Times over an hour are gone. The minimap button's switch is on QoL, General only.
- **Lines one pixel thin at any UI scale.** The window's borders and dividers are drawn exactly one screen pixel, so they no longer come out one pixel here and two there.
- **A smaller window with four tabs (the settings redesigned).** The Headstart window is 980 by 600, down from 1140 by 680. It now has four tabs: QoL (what Headstart does for you), Interface (what it puts on your screen and does to Blizzard's bars and windows), Routes and Character setup. The menu is regrouped. Quests is a page of its own: world marker, quest item buttons, quest mob markers, NPC tooltips, sharing and rewards. Invites moved to Group, the talent window and talent builds to a new Talents page, and General went to the bottom of the menu. Old page names still lead to the right place. On General, the accent colour card no longer keeps an empty row for its reload note; picking a colour says in chat that it shows after Reload UI. A tab at the top clears a search, as a page in the menu does (its results stayed over the other tab's page), and a search no longer starts the swing timer's sample swings on your bars.
- **Settings cards side by side.** A card that holds only settings is half the page wide, and the cards fill two columns instead of a single list with half-empty rows. A card with a preview or a talent tree keeps the full width. Character setup's pages work the same way. A setting whose name doesn't fit beside a wide control puts its name above it.
- **Every feature has its own main switch, in its card's head.** That includes Bank deposit, Mail to alt, Invites, Invite and Leave buttons, Quest sharing, Quest rewards, Reminders, Level-up screenshot, the class trainer, the skins, chat buttons, whisper sound, bag bar and micro menu, window scale, bag position, the talent window and the intro cinematic. For a feature with several settings, switching it off keeps your choices, and switching it back on restores them. On All features every card now has a switch instead of "On" or a name. General's two cards are off All features (they're on General). The Loot log is part of the Loot feed card, since it records while the feed is on. Talent builds: the switch follows the build you're viewing on this character, and the trees stay browsable with it off. RestedXP skin's switch stays off and says why when RestedXP isn't loaded. A Move settings text from someone else doesn't bring the choices a switch keeps while it is off (Invites from anyone, say): only All of it, for a text of your own, does.
- **All features: groups fold.** Click a group's heading (Levelling, Automation and so on) to fold its cards away, and click again to bring them back. Headstart remembers which groups you fold. Each heading shows how many of its features are on, e.g. "9 of 14 on", even while folded.
- **Search lists name matches first.** Settings whose name matches what you typed come before ones that only mention it in their description.
- **Character setup no longer freezes the game while it applies.** Bars, macros, key bindings and game settings now go on a little each frame (three buttons, thirty keys, twenty-five settings) instead of all at once. A new character is set up in a second or two with no lag. A fight pauses it, and it picks up again when the fight ends. Something held on the cursor pauses it too, until you drop it. While it runs, switching profile, undo, Put back everything, Save bars and Save interface wait instead of catching the bars half done. Key bindings change one action at a time, so a fight in the middle never leaves an action unbound. **Set up new characters by themselves** now starts the moment the character logs in, while the loading screen is still up, and does the work in one go where nothing is seen (it waited until a few seconds after the intro, when you could already move and saw it happen). Chat windows, key bindings and the Edit Mode layout wait for the game to load its own first, which would otherwise overwrite them. If the setup is still running when the loading screen goes, the intro plays on until it is done (six seconds at most) and then ends.
- **Character setup: set up new characters by themselves (new; Character setup page; off until you turn it on).** A new character's first login puts your saved bars, macros and interface on at once - what Apply both does - without the window. With no bars saved for its class nothing is put on and the window opens as before; with no interface saved the bars go on alone and chat says so; where it would take something off it asks first. It all happens under the login's loading screen, before you can move. No reload is asked: everything works without one, and a /reload when it suits you finishes it (the line in chat says so). `/ysetup debug` shows when it ended. Your main's macros that no class owns - an emote, /sit, /reload, an item by its ID - are made as account macros, once, so the next new character of any class finds them and makes none (a macro that casts or shows a spell stays the character's own; /ysetup undo leaves the account's ones, as they serve every character).
- **Mail to alt marks what it would send.** At an open mailbox, before you press Send, a small mailbox sits on each item in your bags that Send would mail to your alt; they go as you press it. Not when everything is sent as the mailbox opens, and only on Blizzard's own bag windows (a bag addon's own bags get none).
- **Bank deposit is a Deposit button: nothing goes to the bank by itself.** It deposited as the bank opened, so mats you took out went back in the next time you opened it. Now the Deposit button under the bank window (the "Bank mats" button, renamed) does it on a click, and only then. It waits while an item is still moving or on your cursor - clicking into a move under way could leave an item stuck as "Item is locked" until a relog - and gives up after a while, saying why. The "Deposit when the bank opens" switch is gone; the Bank deposit card turns the button on and off.
- **Glimmer: quest objects are larger.** The crates, plants and totems a quest needs are drawn half as large again as herbs, ore and chests.
- **Glimmer: herbs light up only with Herbalism, ore only with Mining.** A miner's walk was lit by every flower on the way. Where the game won't say which professions you have, both still light up as before.
- **Class trainer: saves for the route's next buy (new, on unless turned off).** On one of Headstart's routes, "If I can afford it" spells now also leave you the price of the next thing the route buys - a weapon at a vendor ahead that you don't have yet - with your Money to save on top. On a Night Elf Rogue in Dolanaar, Gouge was trained and then the Stiletto couldn't be bought. Chat says what the spells were left for; "Always" spells are learned as before, and Learn them under the trainer's window still buys them now. The switch is "Save for the route's next buy" on the Class trainer page.
- **Macro helper: Stealth that stays on is `/cast [nostealth] Stealth`.** It made `/cast !Stealth`, which in Forever took Stealth off again when pressed twice. Prowl the same. A macro made before keeps its text: make it again, or change `!Stealth` to `[nostealth] Stealth`.
- **Talent window: never larger than fits your screen.** Set larger than that, it was held at the top of the screen and couldn't be moved up or down. Now a size above Blizzard's own is made only as large as fits, with a little room to move it; smaller sizes are as you set them.
- **Combat bars: the yards to your target from your own spells too, and as a text of their own (Range text, new).** The range bar's yards came only from asking whether items would reach, and the game often doesn't answer for an item you don't carry, so only "Out of range" showed. Now your own spells that reach from you on are asked too. The range bar also says **Cast range**, in a colour of its own, past melee but within your spells' reach, and made narrow it shows the yards alone (they and "Out of range" were written over each other). **Range text** puts the yards on screen on their own, at a size you set, where you like: unlock the bars and drag it, or use Arrange. `/headstart range` says what the game answered. The ranged swing bar is in Arrange for anyone with a bow, gun or wand, not only for a hunter.
- **Talents before level 10 (new; Bars & windows, Talent window; on unless you turn it off).** Your talent key (N) opens the talent tree before level 10 too, to look at and plan; Blizzard opens it only from 10, and the key did nothing before. The points to spend still come at 10.
- **Upgrades: a profession's tool is never offered.** A Mining Pick, a Blacksmith Hammer, a Skinning Knife, an Arclight Spanner (and Forever's own hammers and knife) and fishing poles could be offered as a better weapon to equip. Not any more, however they score; and one you choose to wear is measured like any weapon, so nothing weaker than it is offered either.
- **Combat bars, cast bar: Blizzard's bar no longer flashes up on a cancelled cast.** Blizzard's own bar was see-through while Headstart's showed, but a cast cut short makes it hold up and fade out by animation, and an animation's alpha is the bar's while it plays. Now those fades go from nothing to nothing while Headstart's cast bar stands in for it, and are put back as they were when it doesn't.
- **Skip cinematics is Skip intro cinematic: a new character's opening flight, and nothing else.** It ended every cinematic that could be cancelled; now only the one a level 1 character logs in to, and on any other character nothing listens for a cinematic at all.
- **The intro cinematic's skip no longer brings a second loading screen.** A new character's opening flight starts while the login's loading screen is still up, and Character setup ended it right then: the world was loaded again behind a second loading screen. Now a cinematic is ended only once the login's loading screen has gone - the same call Escape makes, at the moment Escape could - and only while one is running.
- **Level-up stats in chat is gone: the game writes them itself again.** From build 1.60.1.70425 Forever's chat says what a ding gave you - hit points and mana, talent points, and each stat, Spirit too - as Classic did. Headstart's own lines, there because Forever said only "Congratulations, you have reached Level 2!", would have said each twice: the switch (Reminders page) and its lines are taken out.
- Checked against build 1.60.1.70425: nothing else Headstart uses changed - the game's documented functions and events, the trainer window (it groups its spells now; Headstart buys by the game's own numbering), the chat windows' functions, the bags, the cast bar, the minimap's addon list (Headstart is in it) and the Interface number (16001).
- **A new character starts on a RestedXP guide, and you pick which (Character setup, Interface tab).** Without Headstart's routes a new character's RestedXP was blank: RestedXP marks a default in its menu but loads none by itself. Now each race has its guide, for the account: the one you pick from every guide RestedXP has loaded that begins at level 1 - or, not picked, Headstart's opener for the race where the routes are installed (the first one that is for the character's class), or else RestedXP's own free starter for the race (Northshire, Shadowglen, Coldridge Valley, Durotar, Tirisfal Glades, Mulgore). Still only for a character at level 5 or below, and only with "RestedXP guide for a new character" on. A picked guide that RestedXP no longer has, or one for other classes only (a Rogue's opener picked for Night Elves, on a Night Elf Druid), gives that character the default instead of an empty guide; the choice says which guides are for some classes only.
- `/headstart` help says what `/headstart start` is now: the tour of Headstart's window.

## 0.10.0-beta7

- **Combat bars: which bars show is each character's own.** The swing timer, health, power, cast bar, combo points and range bar were the account's: turned on for a warrior, they showed on your mage too. Now each character keeps its own, and one that hasn't chosen starts with what the last character of its class chose (or, for a class none has chosen for yet, with what the account had).
